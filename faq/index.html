What is blue butterfly pea flower tea?+

Blue butterfly pea flower tea is made from the dried petals of Clitoria ternatea, a plant native to Southeast Asia and parts of India. The flowers produce a vivid cobalt-blue brew rich in anthocyanin antioxidants — the same class found in blueberries.

Nevisan's Blue Flower Green blends these petals with whole-leaf Assam green tea, creating a cup that is visually striking and nutritionally interesting.

Why does blue flower tea turn purple when you add lemon?+

The colour change is a natural pH reaction. The anthocyanins in butterfly pea flower act as natural pH indicators — they are blue in neutral pH and shift to violet and then pink as acidity increases. Adding lemon juice (acidic) triggers this shift instantly. It is the same chemistry behind red cabbage changing colour with vinegar. No dyes, no tricks — pure botany.

What are the benefits of Blue Flower Green Tea?+

Blue butterfly pea flower is rich in anthocyanins — powerful antioxidants linked to cognitive function, reduced oxidative stress and mild anti-anxiety effects. Some research points to proanthocyanidins helping with skin elasticity and eye health.

Combined with green tea, you also get EGCG, L-theanine and moderate caffeine for sustained focus.

Is Blue Flower Green Tea safe to drink?+

Yes. Butterfly pea flower has been consumed as a traditional beverage in Southeast Asia for centuries. It is considered safe for most adults. People on blood thinners or anticoagulant medication should consult a doctor before consuming regularly, as some research suggests mild anticoagulant properties in high doses.

What does Blue Flower Green Tea taste like?+

Mildly earthy and slightly woody from the butterfly pea petals, with the grassy freshness of Assam green tea underneath. The flavour is subtle — the visual impact is more dramatic than the taste. Is spearmint tea good for PCOS?+

Yes — it is one of the few herbs with direct clinical research on PCOS. A trial published in Phytotherapy Research (2010) found that women with PCOS who drank two cups of spearmint tea daily for 30 days showed significant reductions in free testosterone and LH levels.

The mechanism: spearmint inhibits 5-alpha reductase, the enzyme that converts testosterone to its more potent DHT form. It is not a standalone treatment for PCOS, but it is a well-researched supportive addition.

How much spearmint tea should I drink for hormonal balance?+

The clinical research used 2 cups daily for 30 days. Consistency matters more than quantity — drinking it occasionally will not produce measurable hormonal changes. Most customers who track skin changes notice results between 4-6 weeks of daily consumption.

Does spearmint tea help with acne and skin?+

Yes — by reducing excess androgens (specifically free testosterone and DHT), spearmint can help with hormonal acne, oily skin and facial hair in women. This is especially relevant for women with PCOS or androgen-related skin concerns. Results typically show in 4-8 weeks of consistent daily drinking.

What is the difference between spearmint and peppermint?+

Spearmint is milder, sweeter and lower in menthol than peppermint. Peppermint has a sharp, intense cooling sensation from high menthol content. Spearmint is gentler and more pleasant to drink as a daily tea. The anti-androgenic research is also specific to spearmint — peppermint does not have the same hormonal effects.

Can spearmint tea affect testosterone in men?+

The research on testosterone reduction in spearmint tea is specifically from studies on women with PCOS where testosterone was elevated above normal. In men with normal testosterone levels, 1-2 cups daily is not associated with clinically significant hormonal changes. If you have concerns, consult your doctor before drinking it very regularly.

What does Spearmint Green Tea taste like?+

Bright, cool and refreshing with a gentle sweetness. Much milder than peppermint — there is no sharp menthol blast. The green tea base adds a light grassy note underneath. It brews a clear pale green-gold colour. Easy to drink without sweetener, but a small amount of honey rounds it beautifully.

Is spearmint tea safe during pregnancy?+

Spearmint in small amounts is generally considered low-risk during pregnancy. However, because it may have mild hormonal effects, we recommend consulting your doctor before drinking it regularly during pregnancy or breastfeeding. Occasional cups are likely fine; daily consumption throughout pregnancy should be cleared with a healthcare professional.

GABA Oolong Tea
6 questions
What is GABA oolong tea?+

GABA oolong is processed in a nitrogen-rich, oxygen-free environment — a technique developed in Japan in the 1980s. This anaerobic processing triggers a biochemical reaction that increases GABA content in the leaves by 4-10 times compared to regular tea.

GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is your brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter — the signal that tells your nervous system to slow down and stop over-firing.

Is GABA tea good for sleep?+

Yes — studies associate regular GABA tea consumption with improved sleep quality, reduced time to fall asleep and fewer night-time wake-ups. Unlike chamomile, GABA tea produces calm without sedation — you can drink it in the early evening while still remaining mentally present. It is better for people who want to wind down without falling asleep.

Does GABA tea help with anxiety?+

Research supports GABA supplementation for mild anxiety reduction. Since low GABA is directly associated with anxiety and mental restlessness, regularly replenishing it through GABA tea may help manage background anxiety. The effect is gradual — most people notice a difference after 2-3 weeks of daily consumption rather than immediately.

What is the difference between GABA tea and chamomile for sleep?+

Chamomile is more sedating — it promotes sleep directly and is better if your primary goal is falling asleep faster. GABA tea is more calming without sedation — it is better for reducing daytime anxiety, quieting mental chatter in the evening while remaining functional, and improving sleep quality over time. Many people use both — chamomile at bedtime, GABA oolong earlier in the evening.

Is GABA oolong good for blood pressure?+

Yes — several studies have linked regular GABA tea consumption to modest but meaningful reductions in systolic blood pressure. The mechanism is GABA's role in modulating the autonomic nervous system. If you are on blood pressure medication, inform your doctor before adding GABA tea regularly as the combination may enhance the effect.

What does GABA Oolong taste like?+

Smooth, slightly floral and more complex than green tea. Oolong sits between green and black tea in oxidation, giving it a character that is rounded and mellow with less of the grassy sharpness of green tea. GABA processing adds a subtle earthiness. It brews a warm amber-gold colour and is naturally sweet enough to drink without any additions.

Brewing & Storage Guide
8 questions
What temperature should I brew Nevisan teas at?+

Green teas: 80-85°C. Chamomile Green: 75-80°C. GABA Oolong: 85-90°C. Never use boiling water (100°C) for green tea — it destroys L-theanine and makes the tea bitter. The simplest method: boil water and let it sit for 3-4 minutes before pouring over the leaves.

How long should I steep Nevisan teas?+

2-3 minutes for the first steep. If you prefer stronger tea, steep for 3 minutes maximum — beyond this, tannins make green tea bitter. For the second steep, add 30-60 seconds. For a third steep, add another minute. The key is restraint — under-steep slightly and you can always steep again.

Can I re-steep Nevisan teas?+

Yes — whole-leaf teas are designed for multiple steeps. First steep: bold, aromatic top notes. Second steep: softer, more rounded body. Third steep: subtle sweetness. Let the leaves rest 60 seconds between steeps before adding fresh water. Do not squeeze or press the leaves — it releases bitterness.

How much tea should I use per cup?+

Use 1 teaspoon (approximately 2-2.5g) per 200ml of water. This is the standard ratio. For a stronger cup, increase to 1.5 teaspoons — do not extend the steeping time, as that just adds bitterness. For larger vessels like a teapot, scale proportionally.

Can I add milk or sugar to Nevisan teas?+

Milk is not recommended — milk proteins bind to catechins and reduce antioxidant absorption significantly. Honey is the best sweetener — add after brewing as heat degrades honey's beneficial compounds. Lemon works beautifully with Lemongrass and dramatically with Blue Flower (causes a colour change). Rock sugar (mishri) is another traditional option.

How should I store Nevisan tea?+

Store in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight, heat and strong odours. Tea absorbs smells from its environment — keep it away from spices, coffee and other strong-smelling foods. An airtight container is ideal. Refrigeration is not necessary and can introduce moisture. Shelf life is 24 months from manufacture date.

Can I make iced tea with Nevisan teas?+

Yes — and whole-leaf teas make excellent iced tea. Two methods: hot brew then chill (brew double-strength, pour over ice), or cold brew (steep in cold water for 6-8 hours in the refrigerator). Cold brew produces a very smooth, naturally sweet result with less bitterness. Blue Flower Green Tea makes a spectacular iced drink — add lemon for the colour change.

What is the shelf life of Nevisan tea?+

24 months from the date of manufacture when stored correctly (cool, dry, away from light and strong odours). The date is printed on the pack. Whole-leaf tea does not go "bad" after this date but its flavour, aroma and antioxidant content gradually diminish. Consume within 6 months of opening for best flavour.

How do I know if green tea is real and not fake?+

Four signs of real whole-leaf green tea: 1. It looks like actual leaves, not uniform pellets or dust. 2. It smells grassy and fresh when you open the pack — not artificial. 3. It brews pale gold or green, not dark brown. 4. The brand specifies a single origin — garden name, region — not just "premium Himalayan tea". What is PGS-India organic certification?+

PGS stands for Participatory Guarantee System — India's official organic certification framework for small and medium farms. It is recognised by APEDA (Agricultural & Processed Food Products Export Development Authority) and is equivalent in rigour to third-party organic certification for the Indian domestic market.

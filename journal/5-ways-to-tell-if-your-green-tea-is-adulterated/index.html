5 Ways to Tell If Your Green Tea Is Adulterated

2025-11-22  ·  Nevisan Journal

The first sign is the smell. Real green tea smells like grass, hay, and maybe a hint of chestnut. If it smells like flowers, fruits, or anything else — that's artificial flavoring. Tea doesn't naturally smell like mango or jasmine. Those are essences sprayed on low-quality leaves to hide their mediocrity.

The second sign is the color. A good green tea brew is pale yellow to light green. If your tea turns dark green or brown immediately, it's either over-steeped or — more likely — it's not pure green tea. Some brands mix in black tea or use coloring agents to make it look 'premium'.

The third sign is the sediment. Whole-leaf tea leaves unfurl when you brew them. You can see the leaf shape. If your cup is full of fine powder or dust, you're drinking broken-leaf or even tea dust — the lowest grade. That's not a sign of 'strong' tea. It's a sign of cheap tea.

The fourth sign is the aftertaste. Real green tea has a clean, slightly sweet aftertaste. If it leaves a metallic or bitter aftertaste that lingers for minutes, it could be contaminated with heavy metals or pesticides. This is common in teas that don't carry proper certification.

The fifth sign is the price. If a 50g pouch of 'organic' green tea costs less than ₹200, something is wrong. Real organic tea costs more to produce — certification, labor, and quality control all add up. A ₹150 'organic' tea is almost certainly not organic. It's just labeled that way.

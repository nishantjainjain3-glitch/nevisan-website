# The Art of Tea Tasting: A Beginner's Guide

Tea tasting is a sensory experience that goes far beyond simply drinking a cup. Like wine or coffee, tea has nuanced flavours, aromas, and textures that reveal themselves when you pay attention. This beginner's guide will help you develop your palate and appreciate Nevisan's whole-leaf Assam teas like a connoisseur.

## What You Need

- **A clean palate** — avoid strong foods, smoking, or strong fragrances before tasting
- **Fresh, filtered water** — hard or heavily chlorinated water dulls tea flavour
- **Proper temperature** — 80–85°C for green teas, 85–90°C for oolongs
- **A neutral cup** — white or clear ceramic lets you see the liquor colour
- **A timer** — precision matters in tea tasting
- **Whole-leaf tea** — broken leaves release flavour too quickly and unevenly

## The Five Steps of Tea Tasting

### 1. Visual Assessment
Look at the dry leaves first. Whole-leaf Nevisan teas should be:
- **Intact** — not broken or crushed
- **Vibrant** — green teas should be bright green, oolongs should have a warm amber-green hue
- **Uniform** — consistent leaf size indicates careful processing

After brewing, examine the **liquor** (the liquid):
- **Colour** — should be clear, not cloudy
- **Clarity** — whole-leaf tea produces a bright, clear liquor
- **Strength** — should be golden-green for green teas, amber-gold for oolongs

### 2. Aroma
Smell the dry leaves first, then the wet leaves after brewing, and finally the liquor. Good tea has:
- **Complex aroma** — multiple layers, not just one note
- **Freshness** — no stale or musty smells
- **Origin character** — Assam teas should have a malty, earthy undertone

### 3. First Sip
Take a small sip and let it coat your tongue. Pay attention to:
- **Body** — is it light, medium, or full-bodied?
- **Texture** — is it smooth, silky, or astringent?
- **Temperature** — best tasted at 60–70°C, not scalding

### 4. Flavour Profile
Identify the flavour notes:
- **Grassy** — classic Assam green tea character
- **Malty** — depth and sweetness from the Brahmaputra valley soil
- **Citrusy** — lemongrass adds bright, zesty notes
- **Minty** — spearmint provides cool, clean freshness
- **Earthy** — tulsi and GABA oolong have grounding, herbal notes
- **Floral** — chamomile and blue flower add sweet, aromatic layers
- **Spicy** — ginger adds warm, zingy heat
- **Smoky** — whiskey tea has woody, peated character

### 5. Finish (Aftertaste)
The finish is what remains after you swallow. Good tea has:
- **Length** — the flavour should linger for 10–20 seconds
- **Sweetness** — a natural sweet finish indicates quality
- **Cleanliness** — no bitter or metallic aftertaste

## Tasting Tips for Beginners

1. **Start with one tea at a time** — don't mix varieties when learning
2. **Use the same brewing parameters** each time for consistency
3. **Take notes** — write down flavour, aroma, body, and finish
4. **Compare steeps** — whole-leaf tea changes character from steep 1 to steep 3
5. **Taste with others** — sharing impressions helps develop your palate
6. **Be patient** — palate development takes time, like any skill

## Common Tasting Mistakes

- **Using boiling water** — destroys L-theanine and makes tea bitter
- **Over-steeping** — releases excess tannins, creating astringency
- **Using a flavoured cup** — neutral ceramic is essential
- **Tasting on a full stomach** — dulls flavour perception
- **Rushing** — good tea deserves attention and time

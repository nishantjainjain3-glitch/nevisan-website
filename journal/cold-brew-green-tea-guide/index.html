Cold Brew or Iced Green Tea? Start with the Pack Guide

Learn the difference between cold infusion and chilled hot-brewed tea, how to check cold-brew suitability and how to avoid a watery iced cup.

By Nevisan Tea · Updated 29 October 2026

Cold brew describes the preparation, not the serving temperature

Cold-brew tea is infused in cold water. Iced tea can be brewed hot, strained and then served cold. These methods are not interchangeable instructions for every loose-leaf product. Nevisan’s current product guidance gives hot-water preparation; it does not establish a tested cold-infusion time or refrigerated shelf life.

For a Nevisan cup served cold, start with the pouch’s hot-brew method and chill the strained drink promptly. Do not copy an overnight cold-water recipe onto a blend whose manufacturer has not confirmed that use.

Why cold-brew suitability matters

CVUA Karlsruhe’s food-safety explanation of cold-brew tea notes that cold water does not kill microorganisms that may be present. Refrigeration is not a substitute for product-specific preparation instructions. Tea containing dried flowers or herbs should not be assumed suitable merely because another brand sells a cold-infusion product.

If you specifically want cold infusion, ask the manufacturer whether the exact product is intended for it, and follow its water, time, temperature and storage directions. We do not supply a universal six-to-eight-hour recommendation for the Nevisan collection.

Make a small chilled hot brew

  1. Use clean equipment, potable water and a heat-safe brewing vessel. Keep the dry pouch away from splashes.
  2. Measure one portion of tea and record the water volume you normally enjoy. Use the pouch’s temperature and steep time.
  3. Strain all leaves into a clean heat-safe container. Do not keep steeping while the drink cools.
  4. Chill promptly; do not leave a large warm jug standing on the counter. Make a small batch for the occasion rather than preparing days of drinks.
  5. Serve with clean ice made from drinking water. Add citrus only after tasting the plain tea.

This is a serving suggestion using the published hot-brew directions, not a validated preservation process. Follow any stricter storage or preparation instructions on your physical packet.

Fix dilution without oversteeping

Ice adds water as it melts. If your first glass tastes weak, use less ice or adjust the amount of water next time while noting the change. Increasing the steep time far beyond the pouch guide can make the tea brisker rather than solving dilution.

For a direct comparison, split one strained brew into two clean cups: taste one warm and chill the other. Note differences in aroma and strength without assuming a colder cup contains a particular amount less caffeine. Our lemongrass iced-tea recipe applies the same method to a citrus-style blend.

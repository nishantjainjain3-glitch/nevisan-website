Lemongrass Iced Green Tea: Brew Hot, Serve Chilled

Make a small lemongrass iced tea using Nevisan’s hot-brew guide, with clear measures, chilling steps and ways to adjust ice and lime.

Use the hot-brew guide for this iced tea

This is a chilled serving suggestion for Nevisan Lemongrass Green Tea, which contains green tea and dried lemongrass stalks. It is not a cold-infusion recipe. The catalog supplies hot-water directions, so this version brews hot and strains before chilling. We do not claim a tested overnight cold-brew method for this pouch.

The flavour is bright citrus with a gentle grassy finish. That makes it an option when you enjoy lemon-style aromas but want to taste the green-tea base too. Try the first glass plain before adding lime; citrus in the aroma does not mean lemon juice is already an ingredient.

Set out a small batch

  • 4–5g Nevisan Lemongrass Green Tea, using the pouch’s 2–2.5g per-cup guidance for two portions.
  • 400ml potable water as a measured two-portion serving suggestion, plus clean drinking-water ice for serving.
  • A roomy strainer, timer, thermometer or temperature-controlled kettle, and a clean heat-safe vessel.
  • A small lime wedge, optional, to add after tasting.

The 400ml volume is a recipe choice, not a claim that every pouch defines a cup as 200ml. Change the batch size to the amount you plan to serve, and record the water and leaf weights together.

Brew, strain and chill

  1. Heat the water to 85–90°C and pour over the measured leaves. Follow the physical pouch if its directions differ.
  2. Set a timer for 1.5–2 minutes. Keep the leaves free to open rather than compressing them in a small ball infuser.
  3. Strain completely into a clean heat-safe container. Chilling should not become extra steeping time.
  4. Chill promptly and keep refrigerated until serving; do not leave a warm jug on the kitchen counter for hours.
  5. Pour a fresh portion over ice. Taste before adding a small squeeze of lime or any sweetener.

Make a small amount for the occasion. We have not validated a storage life for homemade batches, and a clear appearance or pleasant smell does not establish safety. Use the product’s preparation and storage guidance.

Adjust the glass, not every variable

If the glass is watery, try less ice or a smaller water volume on the next batch. If it is too strong, dilute the strained tea with drinking water. If lime overwhelms the tea, leave it out and compare the plain cup first. Keep the steep time within the pouch guide before changing several settings at once.

Lemongrass Green Tea contains caffeine. Serving it cold does not make it caffeine-free, and this recipe does not establish a caffeine reduction. For the difference between cold infusion and iced tea, read our cold-brew preparation guide. The same page explains why suitability should be confirmed for the exact product.

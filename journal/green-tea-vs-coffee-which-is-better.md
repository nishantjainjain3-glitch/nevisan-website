# Green Tea vs Coffee: Which is Better for You?

The green tea vs coffee debate is one of the most common questions we hear. Both beverages have loyal followings, distinct benefits, and different effects on the body. This guide compares them across key dimensions to help you decide which — or what combination — works best for your lifestyle.

## Caffeine Content

| Beverage | Caffeine per cup | Duration of effect |
|----------|------------------|-------------------|
| Coffee | 80–120mg | 3–4 hours, often followed by a crash |
| Green Tea | 20–50mg | 2–3 hours, smooth come-down |

Green tea contains significantly less caffeine than coffee. More importantly, it contains **L-theanine**, an amino acid that moderates caffeine's effect — producing calm focus rather than the jitteriness and crash many people experience with coffee.

## Mental Clarity & Focus

**Coffee** provides a sharp, immediate energy boost. It's excellent for tasks requiring intense concentration and quick reaction times. However, the crash that follows can leave you feeling drained and reaching for another cup.

**Green Tea** provides a smoother, more sustained form of alertness. The L-theanine promotes alpha brain waves associated with relaxed focus. You stay mentally present without the jitteriness. Many people find green tea better for creative work, reading, and sustained desk work.

**Winner for focus:** Green tea for sustained, calm focus. Coffee for sharp, immediate bursts.

## Physical Energy

**Coffee** stimulates the central nervous system, increasing heart rate and providing a physical energy surge. It's the classic pre-workout or morning kickstart.

**Green Tea** provides gentler physical activation. The combination of moderate caffeine and L-theanine gives you energy without the cortisol spike. Ginger Green Tea in particular is excellent for morning metabolic activation.

**Winner for physical energy:** Coffee for intense physical activity. Green tea for gentle, sustained activation.

## Sleep & Relaxation

**Coffee** consumed after 2pm can disrupt sleep architecture, reducing deep sleep quality. Even if you fall asleep easily, coffee can reduce the restorative quality of your rest.

**Green Tea** is less likely to disrupt sleep when consumed in moderation (2–3 cups before 4pm). GABA Oolong and Chamomile Green are specifically designed for evening relaxation without sedation.

**Winner for sleep:** Green tea, especially GABA Oolong or Chamomile Green in the evening.

## Antioxidants & Health

**Coffee** contains antioxidants, but green tea is the clear winner here. Green tea's EGCG catechins are among the most potent antioxidants known. Regular green tea consumption is linked to:
- Reduced LDL cholesterol
- Lower blood pressure
- Decreased cardiovascular disease risk
- Better cognitive function
- Reduced inflammation
- Skin protection

**Winner for antioxidants:** Green tea by a significant margin.

## Digestive Effects

**Coffee** is acidic and can irritate the stomach lining, especially on an empty stomach. It stimulates gastric acid production, which can cause discomfort for some people.

**Green Tea** is gentler on the digestive system. Lemongrass Green Tea actively supports digestion, while Ginger Green Tea soothes the gut and reduces bloating.

**Winner for digestion:** Green tea.

## The Verdict

**Choose green tea if you:**
- Want calm, sustained focus without jitters
- Prioritize antioxidants and long-term health
- Need gentle morning activation
- Have a sensitive stomach
- Want to improve sleep quality

**Choose coffee if you:**
- Need sharp, immediate energy for intense tasks
- Are physically active and need a pre-workout boost
- Enjoy the ritual and flavour of a strong brew
- Don't experience jitters or crashes

**The hybrid approach:** Many Nevisan customers drink green tea in the morning and afternoon, and reserve coffee for pre-workout or early morning when they need maximum alertness. This gives you the best of both worlds.

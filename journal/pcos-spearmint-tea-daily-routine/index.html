Spearmint Tea and PCOS: Know What Is in Your Cup

Understand the difference between spearmint herbal tea and Nevisan’s caffeinated green-tea blend, the limits of research and how to brew for taste.

By Nevisan Tea · Updated 29 October 2026

Spearmint herbal tea and this blend are different

If you arrived here looking for a PCOS tea routine, first check the ingredients. Nevisan Spearmint Green Tea combines green tea with spearmint leaves. It contains caffeine and is not a pure spearmint herbal infusion. A study of a different preparation cannot establish a result, dose or treatment schedule for this blend.

This page explains that distinction and gives a way to prepare the drink for taste. Nevisan is a tea brand; this guide is not a clinical recommendation or a hormone-treatment programme.

What the small trial can and cannot tell you

A 2010 randomized trial enrolled 42 participants for 30 days and compared spearmint herbal tea with a placebo tea. It reported changes in hormone measurements but no statistically significant improvement in the objective hair-growth rating between groups during the study. That short trial did not test Nevisan’s green-tea blend.

It does not justify promising reduced facial hair, restored fertility or a PCOS cure from a retail tea. The WHO’s PCOS fact sheet describes a condition that needs individual assessment and care. Do not replace prescribed treatment with tea or use a brand’s brewing guide as medical dosing advice.

Build a tea break around taste

If this caffeinated blend suits you, choose a time when you would normally enjoy tea. Set out a cup, a strainer with room for the leaves and a timer. Use the preparation below and remove the leaves at the end, rather than leaving them in the cup through a long meeting.

Try the first cup without sweetener so you can notice the mint and the green-tea finish separately. For the next cup, change only steep time if it is too brisk. There is no required daily number of cups, no benefit countdown and no need to drink a flavour you dislike.

Questions to take to your clinician

  • Does my caffeine intake fit my circumstances and medicines?
  • Am I choosing a drink for enjoyment, or expecting it to treat a symptom?
  • Are symptoms such as changing periods or new hair growth something I should have assessed?

If you need to avoid caffeine, this green-tea blend does not meet that requirement. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, medicines and personal sensitivity are reasons to ask a qualified professional about your own situation. Product questions can go to Nevisan support; medical questions belong with your healthcare team.

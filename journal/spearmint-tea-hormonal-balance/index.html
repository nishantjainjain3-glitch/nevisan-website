Introduction: The Science Behind Spearmint's Hormonal Effects

Spearmint tea has been studied for its ability to reduce androgens in women, making it one of the few herbal teas with direct hormonal research behind it.

Source: Nevisan Tea Research

Spearmint tea is one of the more researched herbal teas when it comes to hormonal health. A clinical trial published in Phytotherapy Research found that women with PCOS who drank two cups of spearmint tea daily for 30 days showed a significant reduction in free testosterone levels and an improvement in LH to FSH ratios. The mechanism is thought to involve spearmint's ability to inhibit 5-alpha reductase, the enzyme that converts testosterone to its more potent form.

For women dealing with PCOS, hirsutism, or androgen-related acne, spearmint tea is sometimes recommended as a supportive, low-risk addition to a broader approach — not as a standalone treatment. The research is specific enough that some functional medicine practitioners include it in hormonal health protocols.

Spearmint is different from peppermint — it's milder, sweeter, and lower in menthol. When blended with green tea, as in Nevisan's Spearmint Green, the result is a bright, cooling cup with a clean finish. The green tea adds antioxidants and L-theanine, making the combination genuinely useful rather than just pleasant.

If you're exploring spearmint tea for hormonal reasons, consistency matters more than quantity. Two cups a day for several weeks is what the research used — not a single strong brew once in a while. Whole-leaf spearmint blended with real green tea is worth seeking out over fannings-based bags.

The Science of Spearmint: How It Works for Hormonal Balance

What Makes Spearmint Special?

Spearmint (Mentha spicata) differs from its close relative peppermint (Mentha × piperita) in several key ways:

  • Lower menthol content: Spearmint contains only 0.5% menthol compared to peppermint's 40%
  • Higher carvone: The primary compound responsible for spearmint's characteristic flavor
  • Milder profile: Less intense cooling sensation, making it more suitable for daily consumption
  • Hormonal specificity: Unique effects on androgen metabolism not seen in other mints

Key Bioactive Compounds in Spearmint

Spearmint contains numerous bioactive compounds that contribute to its hormonal effects:

  • Carvone: The primary monoterpenoid responsible for spearmint's aroma and flavor (50-70% of essential oil)
  • Limonene: A monoterpene with anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties (10-20%)
  • 1,8-Cineole: Also known as eucalyptol, with antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory effects (5-15%)
  • Flavonoids: Including eriocitrin, luteolin, and hesperidin with antioxidant properties
  • Rosmarinic acid: A polyphenol with anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects
  • Tannins: Contributing to the astringent quality and potential antioxidant effects

Clinical Evidence: What Research Shows About Spearmint and Hormones

PCOS and Androgen Reduction

The most well-studied application of spearmint tea is for polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS):

Key Study 1: Akhondzadeh et al. (2007) in Phytotherapy Research conducted a randomized, controlled trial with 42 women with PCOS. Participants consumed spearmint tea twice daily for 30 days. Results showed:

  • Significant reduction in free testosterone levels
  • Increase in luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) levels
  • Improvement in LH/FSH ratio toward normal ranges
  • No significant changes in other hormones like estrogen or prolactin

Key Study 2: Mohammadizadeh et al. (2012) in the Journal of Ethnopharmacology confirmed these findings with 21 PCOS patients drinking spearmint tea twice daily for 30 days, showing reduced free testosterone and total testosterone levels.

Mechanism of Action: Research suggests spearmint works primarily through:

  • Inhibition of 5-alpha reductase: The enzyme that converts testosterone to its more potent form, dihydrotestosterone (DHT)
  • Anti-androgenic effects: Direct blockade of androgen receptors
  • Antioxidant protection: Reducing oxidative stress that can exacerbate hormonal imbalances
  • Anti-inflammatory effects: Reducing inflammation associated with PCOS

Beyond PCOS: Other Hormonal Effects

While PCOS research is the most extensive, spearmint shows promise for other hormonal concerns:

  • Hirsutism: By reducing androgen levels, spearmint may help reduce unwanted facial and body hair growth. A 2015 study in the Journal of Dermatological Treatment found spearmint extract effective in reducing hirsutism scores in women with mild to moderate symptoms.
  • Acne: Hormonal acne, particularly along the jawline and chin, often improves with reduced androgen levels. Spearmint's anti-inflammatory properties additionally help reduce acne lesions.
  • Menstrual Regulation: Some women report more regular menstrual cycles with spearmint consumption, likely due to its effects on LH/FSH balance.
  • Menopausal Symptoms: While less studied, spearmint's cooling properties and mild estrogenic activity may help with hot flashes and night sweats.

The L-Theanine and Hormonal Balance Connection

L-theanine, the amino acid abundant in green tea, promotes alpha brain wave activity associated with relaxed alertness. When combined with spearmint's hormonal balancing properties, this creates a state of "calm focus" – reduced hormonal symptoms without sedation or fatigue.

Combined Antioxidant Benefits

Both spearmint and green tea are rich in antioxidants, but they contain different types:

  • Spearmint: Rosmarinic acid, flavonoids, limonene
  • Green tea: Catechins (especially EGCG), flavonoids

Together, they provide broader antioxidant protection than either alone, helping to combat oxidative stress that can worsen hormonal imbalances.

Optimal Brewing for Maximum Benefits

Water Temperature and Steeping Time

To preserve both the delicate compounds in spearmint and the beneficial catechins in green tea:

  • Temperature: 80-85°C (176-185°F) - just below boiling
  • Steep Time: 2-3 minutes for first infusion
  • Multiple Steeps: 2-3 infusions possible with increasing steep times

Leaf-to-Water Ratio

Standard: 2.5g tea per 150ml water (approximately 1 heaping teaspoon per 5oz cup)

Stronger Effect: 3g per 150ml for enhanced hormonal effects

Lighter Preference: 2g per 150ml for a more delicate brew

Why Not Boiling Water?

Using boiling water (100°C/212°F) can:

  • Degrade some of spearmint's volatile oils (carvone, limonene)
  • Extract excessive tannins from green tea, causing bitterness
  • Potentially degrade heat-sensitive compounds in both herbs

Safety Considerations and Precautions

General Safety Profile

Spearmint is generally recognized as safe (GRAS) for consumption as a food or beverage. Most studies show minimal side effects at typical consumption levels.

Specific Considerations

Pregnancy and Breastfeeding: While food amounts are considered safe, medicinal quantities should be avoided during pregnancy due to potential uterine stimulation

Hormone-Sensitive Conditions: Those with estrogen-sensitive conditions should consult healthcare provider, as spearmint may have mild estrogenic effects

Liver Conditions: Those with severe liver disease should consult healthcare provider before regular consumption

Surgery: Discontinue use 2 weeks before scheduled surgery due to potential effects on blood clotting

Drug Interactions

While generally safe, spearmint may interact with certain medications:

  • Antacids: May reduce absorption of spearmint compounds
  • Diabetes medications: May enhance blood sugar-lowering effects
  • Blood pressure medications: May have additive effects
  • Sedatives: May enhance effects due to mild relaxing properties
  • Liver-metabolized drugs: May affect liver enzyme activity

Always consult with a healthcare provider before combining spearmint with prescription medications.

Quality Indicators: What to Look For

Sourcing and Origin

  • Geographic Specificity: Spearmint grown in optimal climates (Mediterranean, temperate regions) often has higher essential oil content
  • Organic Certification: Ensures absence of pesticides and contaminants that could interfere with hormonal effects
  • Harvest Timing: Peak essential oil content occurs just before flowering

Processing and Freshness

  • Whole Leaf vs. Powder: Whole leaves retain volatile oils better than powders
  • Storage: Should be stored in airtight container away from light and moisture
  • Freshness: Best consumed within 6-12 months of harvest for optimal potency

Third-Party Testing

Reputable suppliers should provide:

  • Heavy metal testing (lead, mercury, arsenic, cadmium)
  • Microbial testing (bacteria, mold, yeast)
  • Pesticide residue screening
  • Essential oil content verification (when making specific claims)

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to see results from spearmint tea for hormonal balance?

Most clinical studies showing significant hormonal changes used a 30-day duration with twice-daily consumption. Some women report noticing improvements in symptoms like acne or oily skin within 2-3 weeks, while more substantial hormonal changes typically require the full 30 days of consistent use.

Can spearmint tea help with fertility?

While spearmint reduces androgen levels which can be beneficial for certain types of infertility related to PCOS, it's not a fertility treatment. Women trying to conceive should consult with a reproductive specialist or healthcare provider before using spearmint therapeutically.

Is spearmint tea safe for men to drink?

Yes, spearmint tea is generally safe for men. However, men concerned about testosterone levels for athletic performance or other reasons should be aware that regular consumption may modestly reduce testosterone levels. Most men won't notice significant effects from casual consumption.

How does spearmint tea compare to other herbal teas for hormonal health?

Spearmint is unique among herbal teas for its direct clinical evidence supporting androgen reduction. Other teas like chasteberry (Vitex) work through different mechanisms (prolactin reduction) and green tea offers antioxidant support but lacks spearmint's specific anti-androgenic properties.

Can I drink spearmint tea if I'm taking birth control pills?

Generally yes, but consult your healthcare provider. Spearmint's mild estrogenic activity could theoretically interact with hormonal contraceptives, though clinical evidence of significant interactions is lacking. Monitor for any changes in effectiveness or side effects.

The research consistently shows that regular consumption of spearmint tea can significantly reduce free testosterone levels and improve hormonal markers associated with PCOS. When combined with the antioxidant and L-theanine benefits of green tea, this creates a beverage that offers genuine hormonal support without the sedation or side effects often associated with pharmaceutical approaches.
